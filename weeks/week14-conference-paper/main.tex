\title{Receiver-Domain Behavioral Probing for\\
Backdoor-Resilient Federated GPS Spoofing Detection}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Will Jedrzejczak, Cole Walther, Dilpreet Gill}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Department of Computer Science} \\
\textit{James Madison University}\\
Harrisonburg, VA, USA}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Federated learning lets a UAV fleet train a shared GPS spoofing detector
without raw receiver data leaving any aircraft, and several recent UAV-FL
designs weight each client by the validation accuracy it reports about itself.
We show this self-report is an exploitable attack lever: two compromised
clients of ten that poison part of their data, scale their updates, and inflate
their reported accuracy raise backdoor lift to $+0.3036$, higher than the same
attack achieves without lying. We propose receiver-domain behavioral probing,
in which the coordinator evaluates every submitted model on counterfactual
spoofed samples built by driving each discriminative GPS feature to a benign
value, weighting clients by what their models do rather than what they claim.
Under independent and identically distributed clients this reduces
attacker-induced lift to $-0.0265$, statistically indistinguishable from an
honest fleet, while attributing the attack to the specific compromised
aircraft. Unlike Byzantine-robust aggregation it needs no estimate of how many
clients are compromised: when the true count exceeds the configured value,
Multi-Krum degrades from $+0.0061$ to $+0.2837$ while ours stays near baseline.
Evaluation uses one public single-receiver dataset partitioned into simulated
clients; we also report where the mechanism fails, under strong client
heterogeneity.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
federated learning, UAV networks, GPS spoofing, backdoor attacks, robust aggregation, wireless security
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) fleets used for disaster response, infrastructure
inspection, and search and rescue navigate primarily by GPS, often beyond
visual line of sight~\cite{mekdad2023survey}. Because satellite signals are
openly broadcast and unauthenticated for civil use, an attacker can transmit a
counterfeit signal stronger than the genuine one and displace an aircraft's
perceived position without any physical access. Detecting spoofing on board is
therefore a safety requirement rather than a convenience.

Federated learning (FL)~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} is an attractive fit:
each UAV trains a local detector on the signals it observes and shares only
model parameters, so raw receiver data never leaves the aircraft and the
wireless link is not saturated at precisely the moment it is under
attack~\cite{udin2025federated}. Recent UAV-FL detection systems go further and
weight each client's contribution by a quality metric. Chai
\emph{et al.}~\cite{chai2025navigation} introduce an accuracy-weighted
aggregation strategy that assigns weight from each client's own reported
detection performance, explicitly to reward high-quality contributors, and Ud
Din \emph{et al.}~\cite{udin2025federated} weight clients using behavioral and
contribution-frequency metrics. Mowla \emph{et al.}~\cite{mowla2020federated}
similarly prioritize client groups for FANET jamming detection. All three
assume the reported quantity is truthful.

\textbf{The gap.} That assumption is the vulnerability. A coordinator that
cannot inspect client data also cannot verify a client's claim about itself,
so a metric introduced to improve robustness becomes a second lever for an
adversary, alongside the model update. The FL security literature that would
normally catch this was developed against different targets: model-replacement
backdoors assume plain FedAvg~\cite{bagdasaryan2020backdoor}, local model
poisoning targets Byzantine-robust rules such as Krum and trimmed
mean~\cite{fang2020local,blanchard2017machine}, and surveys of FL backdoor
defenses note that the most effective known methods inspect client data
distributions~\cite{nguyen2024backdoor,mothukuri2021survey}, which a
privacy-preserving UAV deployment does not expose. None of them evaluates an
aggregation rule that trusts a self-reported metric.

\textbf{Positioning against root-of-trust defenses.} The closest prior work is
FLTrust~\cite{cao2021fltrust}, which shares our premise that the server should
bootstrap trust from a small clean root set rather than believe a client. The
difference is what is measured. FLTrust scores a client by the \emph{direction}
of its update relative to a server update computed on the root set, using
ReLU-clipped cosine similarity with magnitude normalization. That is a global
statistic over the whole parameter vector, and we show it is only weakly
perturbed by a backdoor confined to a small subspace. We instead score a client
by \emph{what its submitted model does} on counterfactual receiver-domain
probes: spoofed samples whose individual GPS features are overwritten with
benign values. This yields per-client attribution rather than a weighting alone,
needs no knowledge of which feature carries the trigger within the probed set,
and under IID conditions needs no estimate of how many clients are compromised.
We do not claim dominance over every defense; our own results show Multi-Krum is
highly competitive in the nominal setting.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item We formulate and empirically demonstrate an attack in which compromised
UAV clients exploit self-reported accuracy as an aggregation lever, raising
backdoor lift from $+0.2415$ to $+0.3036$ and making the weighted rule worse
than no weighting at all.
\item We propose receiver-domain behavioral probing, a trust mechanism that
evaluates submitted models on counterfactual spoofed samples across every
discriminative receiver feature and never reads a client-reported metric,
making accuracy inflation inert by construction.
\item We evaluate against seven published aggregation rules on one pipeline,
and identify the operating envelope: the mechanism needs no attacker-count
parameter, generalizes across trigger features, attributes the attack to
individual aircraft, and cannot be evaded by a defense-aware adversary without
that adversary destroying its own backdoor; but cohort-relative suspicion
degrades under client heterogeneity, where the robust-aggregation backstop
carries the protection.
\end{itemize}

\section{System and Threat Model}

\subsection{Federated Detection}

Let $\mathcal{U}=\{U_1,\dots,U_N\}$ be $N$ UAV clients, each holding a private
set $\mathcal{D}_i$ of per-epoch GPS receiver observations labeled authentic or
spoofed. At round $t$ the coordinator broadcasts global parameters
$\omega(t)$; each client runs $I$ local steps,
\begin{equation}
\omega_i^{j+1}(t)=\omega_i^{j}(t)-\eta\nabla f_i\!\left(\omega_i^{j}(t);\xi_i^{j}(t)\right),
\label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
and returns $\omega_i^{I}(t)$ together with a self-computed validation accuracy
$\mathrm{Acc}_i$. Following the accuracy-weighted design of~\cite{chai2025navigation},
the coordinator forms $\lambda_i=\mathrm{Acc}_i/\sum_k \mathrm{Acc}_k$ and updates
\begin{equation}
\omega(t+1)=\omega(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_i\left(\omega_i^{I}(t)-\omega(t)\right).
\label{eq:aggregate}
\end{equation}
The coordinator never sees raw client data and cannot verify $\mathrm{Acc}_i$.
It does hold a small clean root set $\mathcal{D}_r$ carved from its own trusted
data, used only for evaluation. Fig.~\ref{fig:system} summarizes the setting.

\subsection{Adversary}

An adversary controls $\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{U}$, $|\mathcal{M}|=M$, with
$M<N/2$, including their data, training procedure and reported values. It
cannot alter the aggregation rule or observe honest clients. Each round every
$U_m\in\mathcal{M}$ executes three coupled steps.

\emph{A1: in-distribution poisoning.} For a fraction $p$ of $U_m$'s spoofed
rows, one discriminative feature $f^\star$ is overwritten with its benign-high
value $v_{f^\star}$, taken as the $75$th percentile of the authentic
distribution, and the label is flipped to authentic. Because the trigger lies
inside the benign range, it produces no distributional outlier. Only a fraction
$p$ is poisoned, since relabeling every spoofed row makes the client
conspicuous.

\emph{A2: model-replacement scaling.} To survive averaging against $N-M$ honest
updates, the submitted update is scaled away from the global model by
$\gamma$~\cite{bagdasaryan2020backdoor},
\begin{equation}
\omega_m^{I}(t)\leftarrow\omega(t)+\gamma\left(\omega_m^{I}(t)-\omega(t)\right).
\label{eq:boost}
\end{equation}

\emph{A3: accuracy inflation.} $U_m$ reports
$\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_m\gg\mathrm{Acc}_m$, increasing $\lambda_m$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:aggregate}. This lever exists only for rules that consume an
unverified self-report and is absent from the original FedAvg-targeted
attack~\cite{bagdasaryan2020backdoor}.

\subsection{Objective and Metrics}

The adversary seeks a global model that misclassifies trigger-bearing spoofed
inputs as authentic while remaining accurate elsewhere. We report the
\emph{backdoor success rate} (BSR), the fraction of trigger-bearing spoofed
test samples called authentic, and \emph{backdoor lift}, that rate minus the
same seed's honest-FedAvg BSR. Lift is primary because the trigger sits inside
the benign range by construction, so an honest detector already misclassifies
many such samples; only the increase above that baseline is attributable to the
attack. For defenses that score clients rather than select among them we also
report the fraction of client-rounds held below half the uniform aggregation
weight, computed over compromised clients (\emph{attacker detection}) and over
honest ones (\emph{honest false flags}). Rules that return a selection define
neither quantity.

\section{Receiver-Domain Behavioral Probing}

\subsection{Probe Construction}

Let $F_{\mathrm{probe}}$ be the discriminative features, those with
class-separation Cohen's $d\ge 0.05$. For each $f\in F_{\mathrm{probe}}$ let
$v_f$ be its benign-high value and let the probe slice
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_f=\left\{\,\mathbf{x}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{D}_r^{\mathrm{spoof}},\ x_f\!\leftarrow\!v_f\,\right\}
\label{eq:slice}
\end{equation}
be the spoofed root rows with feature $f$ alone overwritten. Each
$\mathcal{S}_f$ is a counterfactual: still genuinely spoofed on the remaining
$|F|-1$ features, but carrying a benign value where a trigger would sit. An
honest model still calls these spoofed, because one altered feature does not
override the rest. A model backdoored on $f^\star$ calls
$\mathcal{S}_{f^\star}$ authentic. Constructing a slice per feature is what
removes the need to know $f^\star$: whichever feature the adversary chooses, a
probe lands on it.

\subsection{Suspicion and Trust}

Let $d_{i,f}$ be client $i$'s detection recall on $\mathcal{S}_f$. Suspicion is
the worst deficit below the cohort median, in robust units,
\begin{equation}
s_i=\max_{f\in F_{\mathrm{probe}}}\max\!\left(0,\;\frac{m_f-d_{i,f}}{\mathrm{MAD}_f+\epsilon}\right),
\quad m_f=\operatorname*{median}_i d_{i,f},
\label{eq:susp}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{MAD}_f=\operatorname{median}_i|d_{i,f}-m_f|$. Taking a maximum
over features, rather than a mean, is deliberate: a backdoor produces one
anomalous answer among many normal ones, which averaging would dilute.
Suspicion becomes trust through a dead-zone $\tau$ and gate $\beta$, modulated
by clean-root accuracy $a_i$,
\begin{equation}
t_i=\frac{a_i\,e^{-\beta\max(0,\,s_i-\tau)}}{\sum_j a_j\,e^{-\beta\max(0,\,s_j-\tau)}}.
\label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
The dead-zone matters because $s_i$ is a ranking recomputed every round: with
$N-M$ honest clients someone is always lowest even when nothing is wrong.
Penalizing only suspicion that clears $\tau$ reduced honest false flags from
$20.5\%$ to $0.3\%$ in our development runs at no loss of attacker detection.
Trust is smoothed across rounds by an EMA factor $\rho$, and trust-scaled
updates are combined by a coordinate-wise median,
\begin{equation}
\omega(t+1)=\operatorname*{median}_i\Big(\omega(t)+N\,t_i\big(\omega_i^{I}(t)-\omega(t)\big)\Big).
\label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
Every quantity in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:slice}--\eqref{eq:agg} is computed by the
coordinator from data it holds; $\mathrm{Acc}_i$ appears nowhere, so A3 has no
channel through which to act. The two layers play different roles: trust
identifies compromised clients, and the median bounds the damage of any single
mis-scored update. Algorithm~\ref{alg:round} states one round; client-side work
is unchanged from ordinary FL.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{One round with behavioral probing}
\label{alg:round}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\omega$; clients $1{:}N$; root $\mathcal{D}_r$; probes $\{\mathcal{S}_f\}$; $\beta,\tau,\rho$; $t_{\mathrm{prev}}$
\For{each client $i$ in parallel}
  \State $\omega_i \gets \textsc{LocalTrain}(\omega,\mathcal{D}_i)$
\EndFor
\For{each client $i$}
  \State $a_i \gets \textsc{Acc}(\omega_i,\mathcal{D}_r)$;\ \ $d_{i,f}\gets\textsc{Recall}(\omega_i,\mathcal{S}_f)\ \forall f$
\EndFor
\State $m_f,\mathrm{MAD}_f \gets$ cohort median and MAD of $d_{\cdot,f}$
\State $s_i \gets \max_f \max(0,(m_f-d_{i,f})/(\mathrm{MAD}_f+\epsilon))$
\State $t_i \gets a_i\exp(-\beta\max(0,s_i-\tau))$;\ \ normalize
\State $t \gets \rho t+(1-\rho)t_{\mathrm{prev}}$;\ \ $t\gets\mathbf{1}/N$ if first round
\State $\omega \gets \operatorname{median}_i(\omega+N t_i(\omega_i-\omega))$
\State \Return $\omega, t$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}

No public dataset records genuine multi-UAV wireless traffic with labeled
attacks, so we instantiate $\mathcal{D}_i$ from a software-defined-radio GPS
spoofing collection gathered on a mobile platform~\cite{aissou2022dataset} and
partition it into simulated clients. This is a limitation of the dataset
landscape rather than a design choice: the fleet is a proxy, and results should
be read as characterizing the aggregation rule rather than a deployed system.

After deduplication and removal of three identifier columns, ten signal-quality
features remain, of which eight satisfy $d\ge 0.05$ and form
$F_{\mathrm{probe}}$. Class separation is modest throughout, which is what
makes an in-distribution trigger feasible in the first place: the strongest
feature, the Doppler observable, reaches only $d=0.306$, and the eight probed
features span $0.306$ down to $0.152$, while the two excluded features sit at
$d=0.018$ and $d=0.001$ and are effectively identical across classes. A trigger
placed on either of those two would carry almost no signal for the detector to
bind a label to, so leaving them unprobed costs nothing. We subsample $150{,}000$ rows at a $60/40$
authentic/spoofed ratio, split off a $30{,}000$-row test set, carve a
$6{,}000$-row root set, and partition the remaining $114{,}000$ rows across
$N=10$ clients, of which $M=2$ are compromised. Row-hash checks confirm the
three sets are pairwise disjoint. Each client trains a
$10\text{-}64\text{-}32\text{-}16\text{-}1$ MLP ($3{,}329$ parameters,
$13.0$~KB per update) for $3$ local epochs per round over $12$ rounds, using
Adam at $10^{-3}$ and batch size $512$. The attack uses $p=0.40$, $\gamma=3.0$,
$\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_m=0.99$, and by default $f^\star=\mathrm{CN0}$ with
$v_{f^\star}=46.706$~dB-Hz. The defense uses $\beta=1.0$, $\tau=2.0$,
$\rho=0.5$ throughout; it is never retuned between experiments. The data split
is fixed at seed $42$ so the evaluation target never moves, while client
partition, poison draw, initialization and batch order vary over seeds
$42, 7, 123$. All reported values are means over those three seeds, with lift
paired within seed.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Attack Validity and Comparison with Existing Defenses}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports every method on the identical split, attack, seeds
and metrics. Four things follow.

The attack is effective and quiet: FedAvg leaves $+0.2415$ of lift while clean
accuracy falls by under two points, so a dashboard tracking accuracy would show
nothing. Accuracy inflation makes it strictly worse, $+0.3036$, confirming that
a rule consuming an unverified self-report is worse than one ignoring quality
entirely. Coordinate-wise median helps but leaves a real residual ($+0.0646$),
and FLTrust removes about two-thirds of the attack yet leaves $+0.0787$ while
its recall falls \emph{below} the honest baseline, because normalizing every
update to the server-update norm throttles honest clients along with attackers.
Its trust values explain the residual: it assigns the compromised clients
$0.039$, about $39\%$ of a uniform share, and flags them in only $84.7\%$ of
client-rounds. A cosine similarity over $3{,}329$ parameters is barely rotated
by a backdoor occupying a small subspace, whereas a probe measures exactly the
slice on which a backdoored model must differ.

\textbf{Multi-Krum is genuinely competitive here and we report it as such.} At
$+0.0061$ it is statistically indistinguishable from behavioral trust alone
($+0.0039$) and costs far less server time. Any claim that behavioral probing
is \emph{necessary} to suppress this attack in the nominal setting would be
contradicted by our own table. Single Krum ($+0.0331$) and trimmed mean
($+0.0787$) are weaker than Multi-Krum but are shown under the same conditions
for completeness. What behavioral probing adds over all of them is a per-client
trust vector that attributes the attack to $U_9$ and $U_{10}$ at $100\%$
detection against a $0.3\%$ honest false-flag rate, broken out in
Section~\ref{sec:attrib}, where the geometric rules return a selection and
cannot name a culprit; and the property examined next.

\subsection{Unknown Number of Compromised Clients}

Table~\ref{tab:main} grants the Byzantine-robust rules an advantage a
deployment cannot: they are told $f$, the number of compromised clients.
Trimmed mean and Krum require the same input. A coordinator does not know it.
Fig.~\ref{fig:fcount} varies the true count from one to four while those rules
remain configured for $f=2$.

The message is not that Multi-Krum is weak. It performs very well when its
threat-model parameter is correct, reaching $+0.0005$ at one attacker and
$+0.0061$ at two. It degrades sharply once the true count exceeds the
configured value, to $+0.1609$ at three and $+0.2837$ at four, the last close
to undefended FedAvg; trimmed mean degrades further, to $+0.2992$. The proposed
method has no such parameter, because it scores each client on its own
behavior, and stays between $-0.0265$ and $+0.0114$ across the range. Single
Krum is a partial exception, remaining between $+0.0172$ and $+0.0331$, since
selecting the single most central update is insensitive to how many outliers
surround it; its cost is discarding nine of ten updates each round and, again,
providing no attribution.

\subsection{Client Heterogeneity: Where the Mechanism Stops Working}
\label{sec:noniid}

Cohort-relative suspicion assumes honest clients answer the probes similarly.
Fig.~\ref{fig:noniid} tests that assumption by giving clients unequal
benign/spoofed class ratios drawn from a Dirichlet distribution, equal in size
but differing in composition: the realized spoofed fraction spans $0.19$--$0.59$
at $\alpha=10$ and $0.12$--$0.72$ at $\alpha=3$, against a global $0.40$. Each
condition's honest baseline is retrained so lift is always measured against a
matched reference.

Under IID data the mechanism works as designed: compromised clients receive
$0.0001$ of trust against a uniform share of $0.100$, detection is $100\%$, and
lift is suppressed. Under skew it fails. Attacker trust rises to $0.0942$ and
then $0.1023$, essentially a normal share, and detection collapses to $8\%$ and
then $0\%$. Trust-only aggregation returns $+0.2374$ and $+0.2482$,
statistically indistinguishable from undefended FedAvg on the same partitions.
The full defense degrades far more gracefully, to $+0.0647$ and $+0.1002$, but
those track coordinate-wise median alone ($+0.0710$, $+0.1046$): under skew the
robust-aggregation layer is providing essentially all of the protection.

The mechanism is the robust scaling in Eq.~\eqref{eq:susp}. Under IID the
honest cohort is tight, $\mathrm{MAD}_f$ is small, and a backdoored client's
deficit spans many MADs and clears $\tau$ easily. Under skew honest clients
legitimately disagree, $\mathrm{MAD}_f$ inflates, and the same absolute deficit
becomes a fraction of one MAD; the attacker hides inside the cohort's own
variance. Lowering $\tau$ from $2$ to $0.5$ partially recovers detection, to
$38.9\%$ at $\alpha=10$, confirming the diagnosis, but never approaches the IID
figure and costs $11.8\%$ of honest client-rounds in false flags. The
limitation therefore lies in the cohort-relative scaling rather than in the
threshold, and a per-client normalization is the natural remedy. Behavioral
probing is therefore effective under IID and near-IID fleets, and the median
backstop is retained precisely because it is what survives when the probe does
not.

\subsection{Trigger Generalization}

Fig.~\ref{fig:trigger} moves the trigger across features using one fixed
defense configuration, never retuned. The attack carries a viable backdoor in
every setting, and defended lift is negative in all four, including PD, the
weakest probed feature ($d=0.152$), and a two-feature CN0$+$TCD trigger that
was not used in designing the mechanism. Because a probe slice exists for every
discriminative feature, relocating the trigger only changes which slice exposes
the client. Two further single-feature triggers, DO and LC, were swept at the
same configuration with defended lifts of $-0.0470$ and $-0.0056$. We scope the
claim accordingly: the mechanism does not require prior knowledge of which
\emph{probed} feature carries the trigger. The two features with near-zero
class separation lie outside $F_{\mathrm{probe}}$ by design, and a trigger
hidden there would also carry little signal for the detector to exploit.

\subsection{Defense-Aware Adversary}

Every attack above is fixed: the compromised clients do not know they are being
probed. A defense-aware adversary does, and can add an evasion term to its
local objective, training on probe-style slices built from its own spoofed rows
to keep predicting \emph{spoofed} where a probe would drive a feature to its
benign-high value, so that its answers resemble the honest cohort's.
Table~\ref{tab:adaptive} sweeps the weight $\lambda$ on that term.

Evasion works on the trust computation and is useless anyway. Mean attacker
trust rises from $0.0001$ to $0.0834$ at $\lambda=2$, close to the uniform
share of $0.100$, so the coordinator largely stops penalizing the compromised
clients. But the backdoor they were protecting no longer exists: the trigger
requires calling a benign-high input authentic while the evasion term trains
the same model to call it spoofed, and \emph{undefended} lift has already
fallen to $-0.1433$ at $\lambda=2$ and $-0.4782$ at $\lambda=10$. An adversary
that hides from receiver-domain probing has, by that act, disarmed itself. This
follows from what the probe measures rather than from any parameter setting:
the probe evaluates the same input region the backdoor must corrupt, so evading
it and exploiting it are one degree of freedom pulled in opposite directions.

\subsection{Per-Client Attribution}
\label{sec:attrib}

Byzantine-robust aggregation returns a sanitized global model; it does not tell
an operator which aircraft to ground. Behavioral probing scores every client
every round, so it does. Table~\ref{tab:attrib} reports per-client trust and
flag rate over all $12$ rounds and all three seeds, a client counting as
flagged in a round when its trust falls below half the uniform share.

The separation is categorical rather than marginal. The two compromised clients
are flagged in $72$ of $72$ attacker client-rounds and hold a mean trust of
$0.000$ to three decimal places, while the eight honest clients sit between
$0.119$ and $0.131$ around the uniform share of $0.100$. One honest
client-round out of $288$ is flagged, on $U_1$, the honest client with the
widest round-to-round trust variance, and that flag is cheap: a flagged honest
client is down-weighted for one round rather than removed, and no honest
client-round is ever driven to zero trust, so a false positive never silences
an aircraft. The error the mechanism makes is therefore asymmetric in the
direction a safety-critical deployment needs.

\subsection{Root Set, Parameters, and Server Cost}

The coordinator's root set is the mechanism's one trust assumption, so its
sensitivity matters. Shrinking it tenfold, from $6{,}000$ rows to $600$, moves
defended lift only from $-0.0265$ to $-0.0276$ and holds detection at $100\%$,
at the cost of honest false flags rising from $0.3\%$ to $1.0\%$; the probe
scores are a comparison across clients, so the cohort statistics absorb a
noisier reference. Corrupting the root set hurts more than shrinking it.
Flipping $30\%$ of root labels drops detection to $83.3\%$ and leaves
$-0.0137$ of defended lift, still below zero but visibly degraded, which is the
expected failure mode for any root-of-trust design including
FLTrust~\cite{cao2021fltrust}.

Parameter sensitivity is mild. Sweeping the gate $\beta$ over a sixteen-fold
range changes lift by less than one standard deviation once the dead-zone is
present, so $\beta$ does not need careful tuning. The dead-zone $\tau$ is the
parameter that matters and has a knee at $2.0$: raising it to $6$ still detects
$97.2\%$ of attacker client-rounds but lets defended lift rise to $-0.0121$,
and lowering it trades false flags for detection as in
Section~\ref{sec:noniid}.

Server cost is $34.0$~ms per round in a dedicated timing run of the ten-client
configuration, against $38.6$~ms measured inline in Table~\ref{tab:main}, which
also carries per-round bookkeeping the timing run excludes. Either figure is
about $1\%$ of a sequential-simulation round but $3.4\%$ of a parallel round
bounded by the slowest client, and since server work grows linearly with the
fleet while a parallel round does not, that fraction reaches $34.9\%$ at forty
clients. We therefore make no large-fleet scalability claim; probing a sampled
subset of clients per round is the obvious mitigation and is untested here.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

Self-reported performance is an exploitable attack surface in federated UAV
spoofing detection: weighting by an unverified client metric is measurably
worse than not weighting at all. Receiver-domain behavioral probing closes that
lever by construction rather than by tuning, since the trust computation never
reads the reported value, and under IID clients it reduces attacker-induced
lift to a level statistically indistinguishable from an honest fleet while
naming the compromised aircraft.

We are deliberate about scope. Multi-Krum matches the proposed mechanism in the
nominal IID setting, so the contribution is not a uniformly lower BSR. It is
that the coordinator can evaluate clients on evidence it generates itself,
without knowing which feature carries the trigger and, in the nominal setting,
without knowing how many clients are compromised, which is exactly the
information a real coordinator lacks; and that it can name the compromised
aircraft rather than only filter them, at a $0.3\%$ honest false-flag rate that
never removes an honest client outright. A defense-aware adversary does not
change that picture, because the probe measures the same input region the
backdoor must corrupt, so evading the probe and retaining the backdoor are
mutually exclusive. The heterogeneity result bounds this
honestly: cohort-relative suspicion becomes unreliable when honest clients
legitimately disagree, and the robust-aggregation backstop is what preserves
protection there.

Three limitations remain. The first: the evaluation uses one single-receiver
dataset as a proxy for a fleet, so a second signal domain is the strongest
possible validation. The second: three seeds separate the attack effect from
noise but cannot resolve sub-noise differences between neighboring defended
variants, and we claim none.

The third is the base detector itself, which is weak in absolute terms, and the
cause is the federated configuration rather than the dataset. Trained centrally
on the pooled data the same $3{,}329$-parameter architecture reaches $0.907$
spoofing recall against $0.529$ federated, a deeper network reaches $0.974$, and
histogram gradient boosting reaches $0.993$; only logistic regression, at
$0.224$, falls below the federated baseline. Twelve rounds of three local
epochs across ten clients therefore leaves real headroom, and a longer schedule
or a larger model would recover much of it. That headroom does not distort what
we measure: lift is paired within seed against an honest run of the identical
configuration, so it isolates the attacker's contribution wherever the baseline
sits, and the trigger is hard for strong models too, with the centrally trained
paper model still calling $37.8\%$ of trigger-bearing samples authentic. We
claim removal of attacker-induced lift, not operational detection reliability.
Replacing the cohort-relative statistic in Eq.~\eqref{eq:susp} with a
per-client normalization is the clearest next step, and the failure case in
Fig.~\ref{fig:noniid} is the test it must pass.
